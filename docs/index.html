Abstract

PanoVLN explores vision-and-language navigation with panoramic observations. Wider visibility can reveal passages and landmarks outside a perspective camera’s field of view, but simply changing the input offers limited gains. We adapt action prediction, training supervision, and visual representation to make effective use of this wider context.

The policy predicts longer action sequences and uses confidence-guided execution to determine how far to move before replanning. Training routes emphasize branching points and clear route instructions. Semantic and geometric features from RGB panoramas capture scene content and spatial layout without adding visual tokens. With a 4B backbone, PanoVLN reaches 77.3% and 78.0% success rates on R2R-CE and RxR-CE Val-Unseen; quadruped experiments show faster navigation with fewer pauses than prior methods.

Approach

PanoVLN combines Qwen3.5-4B semantic features with PanoVGGT geometry features. Current and historical panoramas support 18-action predictions; confidence determines the executed prefix before the next observation.
